Data and methods

All data are for Toronto’s 585 census tracts, on 2021 boundaries. Official election results and the voting subdivisions (poll catchment areas) were downloaded from the City of Toronto, and election results and census characteristics were each apportioned to these tracts. Everything here describes neighbourhoods, not how individual voters, or any group of people, voted. The code and data used to build this page and its graphics are on GitHub.

  • Elections: poll-level mayoral results, 1997 to 2026, split into dissemination blocks by population and summed to 2021 census tracts. Only election-day votes are included, as advance and mail-in votes can’t be placed in a neighbourhood. The map and the votes Chow added use the polls that can be mapped, about 78% of valid votes in 2023 and 76% in 2026, so the 2023 baseline is not the official result, in which Chow led Ana Bailão 37.2% to 32.5%.
  • Census: Statistics Canada’s 2021 Census of Population, including median age. The chart of the winners’ vote over time uses the census nearest each election, 1996 to 2021; data from 1996 to 2016 were apportioned to 2021 census tracts using the Canadian Longitudinal Census Tract Database (Allen and Taylor, 2018).
  • Correlations: Pearson coefficients across tracts, unweighted, between a candidate’s share of valid election-day votes and a tract characteristic.
  • Boundaries: census tracts from Statistics Canada and former municipalities from the City of Toronto, as in other posts in this series.
